Depiction Coding Study

What you will do

You will see AI-generated images of people at work, one at a time, and answer the same short set of multiple-choice questions about what is visible. The study checks how well automatic tools describe these images, so we need your own independent judgement.

You first code 20 warm-up images that every annotator sees, then one block of about 150 images. The page picks your block from your group, so each group covers the whole set between its members.

  1. Judge only what you can see in this image. Do not guess the occupation or the prompt.
  2. Gender and race questions ask how the person appears to you. There is no correct identity behind a generated image. Pick Can't tell whenever you are unsure.
  3. If several people are visible, answer about the most prominent one. If nobody is visible, answer only the setting.
  4. Hover over an underlined option (or tab to it) to see its definition and examples: FairFace photos for gender and race, example outfits for attire, a diagram for camera angle. Judge the image in front of you, not its resemblance to an example.
  5. Some images are drawings or look unrealistic. Code what you see, and mention it in the note.
  6. Some images may look familiar later on. Answer each time as if it were new.
  7. Shortcuts: number keys pick an option for the highlighted question, Enter saves and opens the next image.
  8. Your answers are saved as you go. Keep your personal link: it brings you back to where you stopped. Please do not share it.

About 30–45 seconds per image, roughly 1.5–2 hours in total. Take breaks.
